Methodological Design & Literature-Based Traffic Thresholds

1. Why 5 km, 10 km, and 20 km Bilateral Polygon Buffers?
Sentinel-5P/TROPOMI has a nadir footprint of 5.5 × 3.5 km (7.0 × 3.5 km prior to August 2019). Buffers narrower than 5 km fall below a single TROPOMI pixel, while buffers wider than 20 km primarily capture interior towns and agricultural perimeters. Separating the Mauritanian side from the Neighbouring side (Senegal, Mali, Western Sahara, Algeria) accounts for the sharp asymmetry in population and road density—particularly along the Senegal River valley (Richard-Toll, Podor, Matam, Bakel).

2. Operational Thresholds for Detecting Cross-Border Traffic Surges:

  • Retrieval Noise Floor (≤ 11.5 µmol/m²): Single-pixel slant-column uncertainty over clean background scenes is ~8.6–9.5 µmol/m² (van Geffen et al., 2020), matching Mauritania's desert road median (~11.1 µmol/m²).
  • Active Corridor Threshold (≥ 15.5 µmol/m²): Corresponds to the 90th percentile of Mauritania's national road network and separates active transport/settlement corridors from background Sahelian soil emissions (Petetin et al., 2023; Qu et al., 2021).
  • Same-Month YoY Traffic Surge Threshold (≥ +15% YoY): Exceeds normal meteorological variability (±5–8%) identified in satellite mobility studies (Levelt et al., 2022; Dammers et al., 2024).
  • Co-Emitted NO₂ / CO Ratio Check: Diesel freight produces high NOₓ relative to CO, whereas savanna/agricultural biomass burning produces massive CO spikes. A true traffic surge is confirmed when NO₂ rises ≥ +15% YoY while the NO₂/CO ratio remains stable or increases.
